# Question Patterns

Use these patterns when variety is needed without losing specificity.

## Scenario and persona
- is [product] a good fit for [persona] who needs [goal] in [environment]
- why would someone with [pain point] look for [product] instead of a basic alternative
- what kind of shopper benefits most from [feature] in everyday use

## Decision drivers
- what should buyers look at first when choosing [product] for [scenario]
- how important is [attribute] if someone cares most about [goal]
- which matters more for [persona]: [factor a] or [factor b]

## Comparison and substitution
- for [scenario], should someone choose [product] or [alternative]
- what type of buyer would prefer [alternative] over [product]
- when does [product] make more sense than [substitute]

## User feedback and friction
- what do people like most after using [product] for a few weeks
- what complaints come up most often with products like this
- what confuses buyers before or right after purchase

## Shopper language
- how would a first-time buyer ask for this product
- how do shoppers describe the problem this product solves in plain english
- what natural phrases signal the need behind this product
