# Scoring rubric

Use this file when the main skill instructions need more detailed scoring consistency.

## Scenario coverage (20 points)

High score signals:
- The title or bullets clearly cover the main Rufus-derived use cases.
- The listing names real situations, not just abstract benefits.
- High-signal scenarios appear early, not buried.

Low score signals:
- The listing stays generic.
- Use cases are missing or too broad.
- The listing mentions features without showing where or when they matter.

## Audience match (15 points)

High score signals:
- The listing makes it obvious who the product is for.
- The audience framing matches Rufus-derived personas.
- Buyer fit is clear without forcing the shopper to infer too much.

Low score signals:
- Audience fit is absent.
- The listing sounds universal even when Rufus implies narrower personas.
- Audience clues are vague.

## Decision-driver coverage (20 points)

High score signals:
- The listing addresses the main comparison factors Rufus surfaces.
- It covers both feature and decision relevance.
- It highlights the factors most likely to influence choice.

Low score signals:
- The listing lists many features but misses the key reasons people choose.
- It over-emphasizes secondary features.
- Important tradeoffs are not addressed.

## Positioning and differentiation (15 points)

High score signals:
- The listing communicates why this option is appropriate relative to alternatives.
- It implies a clear role such as compact, beginner-friendly, premium, or practical.
- It avoids sounding interchangeable.

Low score signals:
- The listing sounds generic.
- No clear “why this one” angle is visible.
- It misses the comparative framing Rufus suggests.

## User-language and GEO semantics (15 points)

High score signals:
- The copy sounds like the way real shoppers express needs.
- Outcome language is easy to map to natural-language shopping queries.
- It avoids unnatural keyword stuffing.

Low score signals:
- The copy is too technical, brand-heavy, or feature-heavy.
- The phrasing sounds like internal product language instead of shopper language.
- Natural use-case phrasing is missing.

## AI-readable structure clarity (15 points)

High score signals:
- Important points are explicit and easy to parse.
- Bullets are logically ordered.
- Features are tied to user outcomes.
- Claims are concrete rather than abstract.

Low score signals:
- Important ideas are implied rather than stated.
- The order feels random.
- Bullets contain feature dumps without interpretation.
- The title is cluttered or unclear.

## Partial-credit principles

Award partial credit when:
- a key point is present but weakly phrased
- a point appears in a low-visibility bullet instead of the title or an early bullet
- the feature is named but the user outcome is missing
- the claim is too generic to fully match the Rufus signal

## Penalty principles

Penalize more heavily when:
- a high-frequency Rufus signal is absent
- a major decision factor is ignored
- the listing emphasizes a secondary point while missing a primary one
- the copy makes the right idea hard for an AI system to interpret quickly
